## What should we wear?

Coordinate without matching. You don't need a new wardrobe.

Start with two to four pieces you already own and like. Build a loose palette
around those. Mix solids with a pattern or two — one or two patterned outfits
against complementary solids almost always photographs better than everyone in
identical solids.

Try to avoid strongly clashing colors, everyone in competing patterns, big logos
or text unless they're intentional, and neon shoes if you can help it.

**Fall:** dark green, brown, cream, maroon, burnt orange, muted seasonal tones.
**Spring and summer:** blues, pinks, yellows. Bright and cheerful is fine — just
not so loud it pulls the eye off your faces.

**Shoes:** clean, reasonably neutral. Don't overthink them; they're often barely
visible.

**Seniors:** clothing and accessories that reflect who they are. A hat, scarf,
sunglasses, a jersey.

**Maternity:** solids photograph particularly well.

**Couples:** coordinate rather than match, and avoid two competing patterns.

**Hair and makeup:** look like yourself. Your normal routine is perfect.

## What should we bring?

**With babies and toddlers:** a favorite toy, a stuffed animal, a meaningful
blanket, a snack, a drink, wipes, and a backup outfit if it makes you feel
better. Go easy on anything that stains right before we start.

This is not a mandatory checklist. Bring what you'd bring to the park anyway.

**Things that mean something:** a grandmother's quilt, an instrument, medals,
baby shoes, special clothing, a meaningful vehicle, sports equipment. These are
the props that matter most and they're always welcome.

**Things I already have:** a white and cream quilt, a wooden children's bench, a
vintage camera, a maternity announcement board, and four-foot lighted GRAD
letters. Optional, never necessary.

## Weather

Grey is not a problem. Overcast light is often kinder than bright sun.

I start watching the forecast about three days out and check in with you as it
gets closer, again around 24 hours, and again the morning of if it's close. Often
the answer is shifting your start time by half an hour rather than moving the
whole day.

If I'm the one who decides conditions won't work, rescheduling never costs you
anything.

## The day of your session

Arrive about five minutes early if you can, dressed and ready to go. If you're
running behind — which happens, especially with kids — just send me a quick
message.

I usually get there ten or fifteen minutes early and I may ask what you drive so
I can spot you. I'll send exact meeting and parking details privately beforehand.

We'll start with introductions, a short walk, and a bit of conversation. If
there are children, that walk is doing real work — it lets them get used to me
before a camera is pointed at anybody. A few test frames, and then we're going.

Session times are approximate shooting time. Walking between spots and letting a
toddler reset are part of it, not deductions from it.

## Restrooms and changing

Outdoor locations may have limited restrooms or none at all. Plan accordingly,
especially with young children — check the Locations page, which lists what I've
confirmed at each spot.

For senior and multi-outfit sessions I have a pop-up changing tent I can bring.
Just tell me ahead of time. Extended sessions have natural breaks built in
between locations and outfit changes.

## Booking, payment and rescheduling

Nothing is due when you book. Payment in full is due by the date of your
session, invoiced through ShootProof.

Life with young children doesn't cooperate with a calendar. Fevers turn up the
night before. Someone doesn't sleep. The morning falls apart for reasons nobody
could have predicted.

I've raised young kids myself, and I'd far rather move your session than
photograph a child who feels awful. With at least 24 hours' notice we'll find
another date, no trouble at all. If something happens the morning of, just tell
me as soon as you can and we'll work it out.

And if the weather doesn't cooperate and I'm the one who calls it, rescheduling
never costs you anything.

## What happens afterward

Your finished gallery arrives within 7–10 business days and stays open for 30
days.

Every professionally edited photograph selected for your gallery is included.
You don't pick a handful and you never pay to unlock the rest. What gets removed
is test frames, blinks, missed focus and duplicates — nothing good is held back.

You get personal printing and sharing rights: print it, make albums, give gifts,
post it, crop it to fit a frame or a social format.

If you significantly recolor, filter or re-edit an image, please don't tag it as
my work — only because the editing style then isn't mine. Ordinary social media
auto-cropping is completely fine.

If something genuinely looks wrong to you, tell me within about 7 days and I'll
take a look.

## Downloading and keeping your photographs

Please download the full-resolution files while your gallery is open. Screenshots
are low resolution and won't print.

Keep them in at least two places, and make one of those somewhere other than
your phone — a computer, an external drive, or cloud storage. Phones get lost.

Long-term storage after your gallery expires isn't guaranteed. If yours has
closed and you need it back, ask me. Sometimes I can help. I can't promise it.

RAW files aren't delivered.
