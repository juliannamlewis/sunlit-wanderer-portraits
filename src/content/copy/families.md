## Your kids don't have to perform

The best family photographs I take usually aren't the ones where everyone is
looking at me. They're the ones a second before or a second after — a kid
whispering something ridiculous to their mom, siblings shoving each other,
somebody being carried who is technically too big to be carried.

So I don't run sessions on repeated *smile at the camera*. I give you something
to do — walk toward me, hold hands, get down on the quilt together, put them on
your shoulders — and then I photograph what happens. We'll still get the classic
everyone-together portrait if you want it, because plenty of people do. It just
isn't the whole session.

I don't move anyone into position with my hands. Everything is talked through.

## What this looks like with small children

Toddlers and babies get photographed doing what they're already doing: walking
with you, being carried, playing, sitting on a quilt, swinging, hanging onto a
sibling, holding the stuffed animal they refuse to put down.

If someone needs a snack, a change, or five minutes to run around, that's part of
the session and not a deduction from it. The clock is an approximation, not a
stopwatch — it doesn't start the moment you park, and walking between spots
doesn't count against you.

## Before you come

Say something low-key to your children. *We're going to the park and Julie is
going to take some pictures while we hang out.* That's it. Try to skip the
build-up, the bribes and the warnings — kids arrive braced for a test when
they've been prepped for one.

## Who's in the photographs is up to you

Blended, divorced, step and every other shape of family is welcome. Tell me
who's important and any groupings you'd like, and I'll take care of it without
making it a conversation on the day.

Larger families of more than seven, get in touch before booking so we can plan
the timing. Regular pricing usually still applies.

Dogs are welcome, leashed. Bring waste bags and treats, and someone who can hold
them if you'd like a few frames without.

## Your privacy is yours

Nothing from your gallery is ever posted publicly — not here, not on social
media, not in anything printed — unless you've signed a release saying it can
be. Children's names and ages are never published at all.

## When to book

Mornings, or the couple of hours before sunset, when the light softens. In
summer I keep midday off the calendar entirely, because overhead sun isn't kind
to anybody.

## Which session fits

The names describe length. What matters is what you want to end up with.

**Short** — one thing, done well. A current portrait for the wall, a few frames
of the kids. If you know exactly what you're after and it's one thing, this is
enough.

**Standard** — the portrait, plus everything around it. Time to move to a second
spot, photograph the siblings together and separately, get mom and dad on their
own, catch the silly ones. Enough variety for frames, gifts and a small album.

**Extended** — when you want a body of work rather than a photograph.

Say you've moved into a house with a lot of empty wall, and it's been two or
three years since anyone photographed your family properly.

Here's how ninety minutes actually goes. We might start at the farm at Carriage
Hill — the barn and farmhouse make a good rustic backdrop, and it's an easy
place to spread the cream quilt out for a picnic and let everyone settle in.
Kids warm up faster when there's something to do. If there's time we wander over
to the bridge and the creek. Then we head to the pond as the light drops, and
that's when we get the more formal frames: everyone together, each child on
their own, mom and dad by themselves for once.

Along the way you get the candids — duck-duck-goose, a kid on dad's shoulders,
another one hugging mom's leg. All the siblings together. The dog in some and
not others.

That's a gallery wall, a holiday card, and enough left over for an album for the
grandparents. You can't get there in fifteen minutes.
