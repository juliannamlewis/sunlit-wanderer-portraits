## I don't photograph weddings

Saying that first, plainly, so nobody discovers it three emails in. Wedding
photography isn't something I offer.

What I do photograph: engagements, surprise proposals, engagement and elopement
announcements, anniversaries, seasonal portraits, and couples who simply want
good pictures of themselves together and never got around to it.

## The goal isn't a nicer selfie

It's affection, friendship, chemistry, and how you two actually are with each
other. Some couples are all over each other. Some are shoulder-to-shoulder and
quietly devastating. Neither one needs fixing.

If you're camera-shy, we start with movement — walking, talking, laughing at
something — before anything more deliberate. By the time we get to the
intentional portraits you'll have forgotten to be self-conscious about it.

## What we might do

Walking. Holding hands. Sitting and leaning into each other. Looking at one
another instead of at me. A picnic setup. Something seasonal. Rings, hands,
details. Your dog, leashed. Anywhere or anything that means something to the two
of you — a place, an object, a story. Bring it.

Posing here is more collaborative than a family session. I'll show ideas, you'll
pick what feels right, and I'll guide it from there. Nothing is ever adjusted by
hand.

## Surprise proposals

Yes, and start early. These need real planning — timing, where I stand, how I
stay unnoticed, what happens in the ten minutes afterward. Message me well
before the date.

## What to wear

Coordinate, don't match. Avoid two competing patterns. Start from things you
already own and feel good in — you'll look like yourselves rather than like
you're in costume.

## Which session fits

**Short** — one announcement-worthy set. Good when you already know the shot you
want.

**Standard** — where most engagement sessions land. Time to walk, warm up, and
get past the self-conscious part before the best frames happen.

**Extended** — two settings and an outfit change. Also the right choice for a
proposal, since it leaves room for the walk-up, the moment itself, and the giddy
half hour afterward, which is often where the best photographs are.
